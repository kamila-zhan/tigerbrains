Being aware of emotions, thoughts, feelings and reality by asking questions.

Do not judge or analyse the answers, but accept and take notes.

Breathe slowly.

*[Getting Started with Mindfulness](https://www.mindful.org/meditation/mindfulness-getting-started/)*

### Examples of being mindful

#### Naming

Identifying and saying the feeling out loud helps to take control of it.  
For example, while feeling irritated, say "I feel irritation".

The reason is that talking activates the part of the brain responsible for reasoning, `prefrontal cortex`, and lowers activity of the stress centre, `amygdala`.

The same is true for thoughts and real situations.  
Saying and naming them helps to initiate control.  

#### Body scan

A body scan is another great way to practice mindfulness.  
It especially helps to relax the muscles, which are surprisingly often tense for no reason, and avoid pain in the neck and shoulders later.

Technique involves scanning your body from toes to head and noticing any sensations.  
It should be done slowly. To help really notice each part, name as much as you can, including inner organs, as you go through them.

*[Beginner’s Body Scan Meditation](https://www.mindful.org/beginners-body-scan-meditation/)*

#### Breathing

Noticing the breathing at random moments and making it slower is part of being mindful too.

Slow breathing activates relaxation in the nervous system, lowers heart rate, blood pressure and helps to calm down.

Breathing technique is the simplest to understand, yet challenging to learn because it is easy to get distracted. 

### Mindfulness & Brain

Mindfulness trains the prefrontal cortex, which manages focus, decision-making and impulse control.

Mindfulness helps the hippocampus, the memory and learning centre in the brain.

Mindfulness makes the amygdala less, and it is an emotional alarm system responsible for stress.  

It is great to know that real changes in the brain occur, so the benefits of mindfulness are broad and long-term.  

As the list above suggests, better focus, impulse control, greater memory, learning abilities and less stress are among side effects of practising mindfulness.

*[How the Brain Changes When You Meditate](https://www.mindful.org/how-the-brain-changes-when-you-meditate/)*

### Using mindfulness in a stressful situation

The very first thing is to breathe.  
Slow the breathing.  

After reaching the state where it seems reasonable to pay attention to other things, move on to identifying and naming emotions, thoughts and feelings.

No need to solve anything, make decisions or perform analysis.
Only observing and saying or even writing down everything happening.  

`Everything is bad` is **not** an example of observation.  
`Emotions: fear..., thoughts: everything is bad..., feeling anxious, the place seems safe and nothing objectively bad is happening` is a kind of good observation which can be logged into an imaginary or real journal. 
 
Try to get distant from emotions and thoughts and stay in an unbiased observing mode.

*[How to Manage Stress with Mindfulness and Meditation](https://www.mindful.org/how-to-manage-stress-with-mindfulness-and-meditation/)*

### Asking questions

Being mindful is also asking yourself questions.

Naming current emotions and thoughts implicitly meant asking questions like:
 - what do I feel right now?
 - what thoughts are in my mind?

To move mindfulness to some future problem or to make some conclusions about past situations, questions can be:
- when that event happened, how did I feel?
- if I have to deal with that task now, what thoughts come to my mind?

If being mindful is being aware of emotions, for example, then asking, what do I feel?, is the way to come to that awareness.

### Where to start

Start by asking questions.

Mindfulness is not easy to learn and practice, even if it seems so.

Direct and simple action like asking a question, `what do I think/feel?`, will help to initiate the process. 

As you progress, mindfulness will hopefully become part of your everyday life and bring all the great benefits it has in it.
