title: Radical Acceptance
---

The idea of radical acceptance comes from Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) and means totally accepting without judgement and fighting.

What we discover while being mindful can be unpleasant to us and cause resistance or denial.  

For example, finding out you are angry at your friend and have thoughts that may offend him/her may conflict with the intention of being a good friend.  
As a quick solution, you may pretend you are not actually angry and ignore your thoughts.  

While seeming like a good solution, there are several pitfalls:  
- Energy is spent on pretending.  
- The initial anger is still in you and may become stronger.  
- If you want to get rid of anger and thoughts, denying them leaves zero chance to do so.

`Acceptance` is the first and unavoidable step on the path of self-exploration and helping yourself.  

Acceptance saves you energy to deal with mental issues, as you do not try to fight real emotions and thoughts.  

As real emotions and thoughts are kept, you have a chance to work through them.
  
Accepting reality is also part of what should be accepted without altering.  
It helps you to see the real situation and deal with it effectively, while trying to see reality as more likeable only gives illusory relief.

Accepting does not mean you like, approve or agree with your thoughts and feelings.  
It is also not about giving up and living with them forever.   

It's a tool to practice mindfulness with less resistance, face facts and reality as they are and have a good foundation for further mental work.  

Just like mindfulness, the skill of radical acceptance takes time to learn and is not easy to practice.    
But as you do practice, your brain will be more and more trained to do that.  
The more you train, the easier it becomes.

`Without acceptance it is hard to see reality, emotions and thoughts as they are`  
`Not seeing reality, emotions and thoughts as they are leaves zero chance to deal with them`
