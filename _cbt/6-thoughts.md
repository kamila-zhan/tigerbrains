Our thoughts are a kind of voice-over of what is happening inside our mind and body.    

We see a friend and think: "Oh, that is my friend."  
We state that our brain recognised a friend's face.  

We become irritated and think: "Why is everyone so slow today?!"  
A hunger, tiredness or anxiety is signalling us and causing irritation.  
We state that in the form of angry comments.

Thoughts can take forms other than words, but to ease understanding and apply CBT techniques, only thoughts as sentences are used.

### Thoughts-feelings theory

Behind every thought we can find feelings.  
Even a simple statement like "the weather seems nice today" can mean a person feels calm right now.  

Behind every emotion we can find a thought.  
You feel a sudden sadness without understanding what caused it.  
But doing a small self-investigation may reveal that you think: "I will never get a job."  
A potential trigger is that you heard someone talking about his job and reacted with this emotion and thought.  

*[The CBT Triangle](https://cogbtherapy.com/cbt-model-of-emotions)*

### Why work with thoughts?

Emotions are a big part of us.  
They show what is important, what our needs are and make our life colorful.

However, in case you want to work through your emotions, it is better to interpret them as thoughts in sentences.  

For example, you want to change fear of conversation to being calm.  
It is hard to know how to do so having only feelings.  
Saying "I am calm" is unlikely to help.  
Breathing methods can help to calm the body, but fear may still come over and over again.   

Find out all the thoughts that are behind the fear.  
Check how rational and true they are.  
Rewrite the thoughts to more accurate and helpful ones.  
Each time you are triggered into fear of conversation, remind yourself to switch into rewritten thoughts instead of old ones.  

With time, a new thought path becomes more and more used and familiar, so switching to it gets easier.  
As you switch, you will get calmer.

This is not a quick method, but it should work.

### Triggers

It is good to not only realise what your current feelings and thoughts are, but understand what triggered it too.  

Triggers can be physical (hunger, sleepiness) or mental (loneliness, need of emotional support).

If you know the trigger, you can find suitable help for yourself by working directly with the cause.

*[3 Steps to Identify What Triggers You](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/cbt-made-simple/202103/3-steps-identify-what-triggers-you)*

### Core beliefs

In CBT, core beliefs are thoughts which are hidden underneath everyday automatic negative thoughts.  
The **downward arrow** technique is used to find them.

A first thought might be:
- "I am failing the presentation."

Ask yourself the following question repeatedly until you reach the core belief, which is usually short and defines you in 1-2 words.
- **If that were true, what would it mean about you?**

"Everyone in the audience will notice I'm stumbling and think I don't know what I'm talking about."  
"They will lose respect for me, and my boss will realise they made a mistake hiring or promoting me."  
"I will be sidelined at work, lose my professional network, and end up completely alone in my career."  
- Core belief: "I am defective and fundamentally unloveable."

Every time you give a presentation, you are actually thinking and believing in that core belief you find with the downward arrow.  

Knowing your core beliefs also helps to choose better what you should work on and understand your emotions.

*[Core beliefs: What they are and how to identify them](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/core-beliefs)*

### Self-talk patterns

We comment on whatever happens to us and everything we do with our inner voice.  
It can be supportive, criticising, rude or loving.  

We hardly notice it in everyday life and are used to it.  
Mindfulness can help to start noticing it and understanding its patterns and tone.  

Self-talking character is mostly formed in our childhood and if we were criticised often, we keep criticising ourselves when we grow up.  

As these are also thoughts, in case you want to improve your inner voice, the same techniques can be applied as in CBT.

*[The Way We Speak to our Children Becomes Their Inner Voice](https://www.kurtzpsychology.com/the-way-we-speak-to-our-children-becomes-their-inner-voice/)*

### Final thoughts

Thoughts represent a lot about us.  
Realising and understanding them is understanding yourself.

They are powerful and can affect your feelings and behavior.  
Handling and controlling them is having a power over your emotions and actions.

Practice CBT techniques and challenge your thoughts.  

When you just start running, it is hard to believe you will run a 42km marathon one day.  
But you **know** that if you practice you will do it eventually, as muscles have no choice but become strong after training. 

`A brain has no choice but to become stronger too.`  
`Keep training.`
